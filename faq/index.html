Frequently asked questions

About renting power banks, and about this map.

What is Powerbank Radar?

A free map of power bank rental stations from every operator we can find, in one place, with prices, opening hours and, where the operator publishes it, how many power banks are available.

How does renting a power bank work?

Find a station near you, then unlock a power bank with the operator's app (scan the QR code on the station) or, at some stations, by tapping a bank card or phone. Take the power bank with you while your phone charges, and return it to any station of the same operator. You pay for the time you had it.

How much does it cost to rent a power bank?

Most operators charge around €1–3 per half hour or hour, with a daily maximum of about €8–10. Some ask for a deposit of €19–30, refunded when you return the power bank. In Israel, Dead Batt charges ₪15 per hour. Each operator page lists its published price.

Can I return a power bank to a different station?

Yes, to any station of the same operator, usually in any city where it operates. You can't return it to another operator's station.

Do I need to download an app?

Usually. Most operators unlock power banks through their own app. Some stations take a contactless bank card instead, for example Dead Batt in Israel and some Juice and Buzz stations in the Netherlands. You don't need an account or app to use Powerbank Radar itself.

Which cables do rental power banks have?

Most rental power banks have built-in cables for USB-C and iPhone (Lightning), and often micro-USB, so you don't need your own cable.

What happens if I don't return the power bank?

The operator keeps charging up to its daily or maximum price, and after a few days usually treats the power bank as bought and keeps the deposit or charges a replacement fee. Check the operator's terms.

Which countries are covered?

The UK, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Spain, Germany, Sweden, Italy, Finland, Greece, Portugal, Cyprus, France, Austria, Denmark, Norway, Israel, Poland, Luxembourg, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Belgium, Iceland, Croatia and Czechia. More countries are being added.

How up to date is the information?

The station list is refreshed every day from each operator's public map. For most operators, the map also shows the current number of available power banks when you open a station.

Is Powerbank Radar free?

Yes. There's no account, no app to install and no fee.

Can I link to the stations near a specific place?

Yes. Add the place's coordinates to the address: https://www.powerbankradar.com/?near=latitude,longitude, for example https://www.powerbankradar.com/?near=52.3731,4.8926 for Dam Square in Amsterdam. The map opens there and lists the nearest stations.

Is Powerbank Radar part of one of the operators?

No. It's independent and not affiliated with any operator. For help with a rental, contact the operator.

A station is missing or wrong. How do I report it?

Use the contact form. Tell us the operator and the address, and we'll check it.

I run a power bank rental network. Can you add it?

Yes, please get in touch through the contact form. A public list or feed of your stations is all we need.
